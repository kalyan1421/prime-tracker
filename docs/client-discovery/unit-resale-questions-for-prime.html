Prime Tracker · Decision memo

Sold, leased, sold again

One unit, three owners, two tenants. The tracker can already tell that story for leases — it stops dead at the first sale.

11 Sept 2026 For the Prime sales team 7 questions · 1 blocking

Two scenarios came up: a unit sold to a business, later leased to a tenant, later sold again; and a lease stopped early so the unit can be re-let to somebody else. Both need to sit in date order on the unit's record.

The second one is already built. The first one is not, and it is not a small gap — it is a question about whose money the rent is, which nobody at Prime has been asked yet.

Already works

Stopping a lease early and re-letting to a new tenant, with the deposit carried across or refunded. Dates enforced so two tenancies can't overlap.

Not built

Anything after a sale. SOLD is the end of a unit's life in the system — no second sale, no lease afterwards that counts.

Waiting on Prime

Whose rent is it once Prime has sold the unit? Three plausible answers, three very different builds.

The decision everything else waits on

Question 1 · blocking

When Prime sells a unit to a business and it is later leased to a tenant, who collects that rent — and does Prime earn anything from it?

Three answers are all plausible for a commercial developer, and they are not variations on a theme. One is a reporting change; one is a new event type; one is a property-management product.

Pick one
ModelRent isPrime earnsEffort
Manage it collected by Prime, paid over to the owner a management fee High
Take it back Prime's own, the unit returns to the book the full rent Medium
Just record it the owner's business, not Prime's commission on the resale only Low · suggested first step

The tracker behaves like the third option today, except it hides the history instead of showing it. Making that one honest and complete is a small job. The other two are worth doing only if Prime actually works that way.

The rest of the questions

None of these block the first step, but each one shapes a later phase.

Question 2

Has a unit ever actually been sold twice, or is this about future deals?

Nothing in the system shows it happening. If it has happened off-system we need the old records; if not, we only need the door opened.

Question 3

Between the two sales, does Prime still own the unit?

If yes, the first sale was reversed or bought back and we need to record that with its own date. If no, the second sale is Prime brokering someone else's asset.

Question 4

Should a resale count in "Closed Sales, all-time"?

That figure reads $32.68M today. Adding resales to it counts the same unit's value twice. Our recommendation is a separate resale line — but it is Prime's number to define.

Question 5

Does Prime take a commission on a resale?

If so, we would track it exactly like the broker commissions already in the system, including split installments.

Question 6

On a past tenancy entered from a spreadsheet, was the security deposit collected?

Past rent is already recorded as paid in full so old tenancies don't show up as money owed. Deposits are not — every imported lease currently reports its deposit as still outstanding. We plan to match the rent rule. Worth confirming: for a tenancy that has already ended, should the deposit read as collected and then returned, or simply collected?

Question 7

When a lease is stopped early and re-let, what usually happens to the deposit?

Transferred to the new tenant, refunded, or put against what the old tenant owed. All three work today — knowing the common one lets us pre-select it instead of asking every time.

How we would build it

The first step needs no answers and is worth doing regardless.

Two smaller things from the same conversation

Deposits on imported leases

A real inconsistency, and a small fix. Imported past rent is marked paid in full on purpose, so a 2019 tenancy doesn't land in today's collections report. The security deposit on the same lease is left as outstanding. Question 6 settles the one detail; the change itself is quick.

Monthly collection detail from your own spreadsheet

Already works when you use the downloaded template — the "Ledger Exceptions" tab reads the months that weren't paid in full and attaches them to the right unit's tenancy, month by month. It does not work in "Upload your own spreadsheet" yet. Extending the column mapper to accept a month and an amount collected closes that, and needs nothing from Prime.
